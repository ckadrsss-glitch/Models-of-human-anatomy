| **28 permanent teeth** (no third molars), separate enamel / root materials | same | CC BY-SA 4.0 |

Z-Anatomy (Gauthier Kervyn, Marcin Zielinski) is derived from **BodyParts3D**, © The Database Center for
Life Science, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan. It is a segmentation of one adult male; it is anatomically realistic
but it is a single individual, not a population average.

Export (`tools/export_skull.py`, run with the official `bpy` 5.3 module — Blender itself is not
installed in the build environment):

* evaluated meshes (Boolean modifiers of the ethmoid applied), world transforms baked, mm units,
  +Y up, +Z anterior; no decimation (79 682 vertices, 159 215 triangles);
* **ambient occlusion baked per vertex with Cycles** (48 samples) on the assembled skull;
* suture faces (Z-Anatomy "Suture-*" materials) flagged and slightly darkened in the shader;
* for every tooth: crown anchor and a rigidity profile (crown rigid, root follows the alveolar bone).

Not used: `Z-Biomechanics.7z` (skeleton with muscle attachments — no additional head structures),
soft tissues, cervical vertebrae, hyoid.

## What is modelled procedurally (not scanned)

The nine skeletal variants are **deformations of the single Z-Anatomy skull** computed on the GPU
(`src/morph.js`). They are schematic, smooth and plausible, but they are not CT segmentations of nine
real patients. See [SCIENCE.md](SCIENCE.md) for exactly what is changed.

## Reference video

`TRG_reference_under_10MB.MP4` (510×1108, 28 s) was studied for composition and sequence only
(title → planes → vertical types → sagittal classes → 3 × 3 grid → outro). None of its graphics,
colours, labels, numbers or the Sassouni plane construction were reused; the film uses its own
